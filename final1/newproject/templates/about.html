Nitrogen

Nitrogen is a key nutrient in crop recommendation systems as it directly impacts plant growth and productivity. The nitrogen values in such systems are used to determine the optimal fertilizer application rates, ensuring that crops receive sufficient nutrients without leading to environmental issues like soil degradation or water contamination. Generally, nitrogen levels in the soil are measured in parts per million (ppm) or kilograms per hectare (kg/ha).

  • Low Nitrogen Levels:

    • Below 20 ppm (or < 50 kg/ha) Insufficient for most crops, and fertilizer application is often required.

  • Moderate Nitrogen Levels:

    • 20 to 40 ppm (or 50 to 100 kg/ha) Suitable for some crops but may still require supplementation, depending on the crop's nitrogen requirements.

  • Optimal Nitrogen Levels:

    • 40 to 60 ppm (or 100 to 150 kg/ha) Sufficient for many crops, leading to healthy growth and good yields.

  • High Nitrogen Levels:

    • Above 60 ppm (or > 150 kg/ha) May lead to excessive vegetative growth at the expense of yield, or cause nitrogen leaching and pollution.

Phosphorus

In a crop recommendation system, the phosphorus (P) levels are crucial for determining the suitability of soil for specific crops. Phosphorus values typically refer to the available phosphorus in the soil, measured in parts per million (ppm). The recommended phosphorus levels vary based on crop requirements and soil types.

  • Low Phosphorus (Deficient):

    • Less than 10 ppm (often below 5 ppm is considered very low) This level is typically insufficient for most crops, and phosphorus fertilization is necessary.

  • Moderate Phosphorus:

    • 10–20 ppm This is considered an adequate range for many crops, but for phosphorus-hungry crops, additional fertilization might still be beneficial.

  • High Phosphorus:

    • 20–40 ppm Soils in this range are generally well-supplied with phosphorus and may not need additional phosphorus fertilization, depending on the crop.

  • Very High Phosphorus:

    • Above 40 ppm At these levels, phosphorus is typically more than sufficient, and further phosphorus application may not improve crop yield. In fact, excess phosphorus can lead to environmental issues like water pollution through runoff.

Potassium

In a crop recommendation system, potassium (K) levels are crucial for plant health and productivity. Potassium helps in various physiological functions such as water regulation, enzyme activation, and photosynthesis. The optimal levels of potassium can vary depending on the type of soil, crop type, and region.

  • Low Potassium Levels:

    • Less than 60 mg/kg (or ppm)

  • Medium Potassium Levels:

    • 60 - 120 mg/kg

  • High Potassium Levels:

    • More than 120 mg/kg

Temperature

  • Cool-Season Crops:

    • Optimal growing temperature: 10°C to 20°C (50°F to 68°F) Temperature below 0°C may damage the plants, while above 30°C can inhibit growth.

  • Warm-Season Crops :

    • Optimal growing temperature: 20°C to 30°C (68°F to 86°F) Temperatures below 10°C can slow down growth, while temperatures above 35°C can cause stress and reduce yields.

  • Tropical Crops :

    • Optimal growing temperature: 25°C to 35°C (77°F to 95°F) These crops thrive in warmer conditions and may be adversely affected by temperatures below 15°C or above 40°C.

  • Heat-Resistant Crops :

    • Can tolerate temperatures up to 40°C (104°F) or even slightly higher.

pH

In a crop recommendation system, the pH value of the soil is a critical input parameter. Depending on the pH value, the system suggests crops that will thrive in that specific soil condition, helping farmers select the most suitable crops or implement soil amendments to adjust the pH for their desired crops.

  • Acidic soil (pH 4.5–6.5):

    • Crops like potatoes, blueberries, cranberries, and sweet potatoes prefer more acidic conditions.

  • Neutral to slightly acidic soil (pH 6.0–7.0):

    • Most crops, such as corn, wheat, rice, tomatoes, carrots, and beans, grow best in this range.

  • Alkaline soil (pH 7.0–8.5):

    • Crops like spinach, beets, broccoli, and cabbage can tolerate more alkaline conditions.

Humidity

  • Low Humidity:

    • Below 40% (suitable for dry land crops like cacti, or drought-tolerant crops such as sorghum).

  • Moderate Humidity:

    • 40% - 70% (suitable for a wide range of crops, including grains, vegetables, and fruits).

  • High Humidity:

    • Above 70% (suitable for tropical crops like rice, bananas, and certain vegetables).

Rainfall

  • Low Rainfall:

    • Less than 250 mm per year. Suitable for drought-resistant crops. Moderate Rainfall: Between 250 mm and 750 mm per year. Suitable for a wide range of crops, including many staple crops.

  • High Rainfall:

    • More than 750 mm per year. Ideal for crops that require a lot of water but might need management to prevent water logging or erosion.
